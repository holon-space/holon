## "Save for later" has no later
https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=frjyb4_4Rlg

### Background information
Arrival: When an item is shown to you.

- Useful: `Later` state that has a date when it pops up again.
- Email is an arrival surface, a place where things land and get processed. What parts of Holon are that? I guess it should be a single one.
- Saving to a list allows you to memorize more, but *iff* you can rely on the system.
- Wanting decays. Every save is a plan for your future self, but your future self does propably not agree with it.
- Saves in a platform is strong signal for the algorithm and deepens your invest.

### How to properly design "Save for later"?
https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=frjyb4_4Rlg&t=747s
* Resurface early (motivation still there)
* Bound the arrivals (only a few items, never the full pile). Don't build a guilt pile
* Let items retire. Resurfaced twice without interaction -> Frame as completion, archive
* Arrival surface. Usually Email, but Holon actually is this for all inboxes. It is the central arrival surface!

### Take-aways
- [ ] Introduce `Later` state that has a date when it pops up again.


## From the To-Do List to the AI Agent

https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=OuEKdD_1F8s

### Background Information

Affordance: what an object lets you do

- A To-Do list is a list of trades
- Paper has multiple affordances
  - Spread on desk
  - See at a glance
  - Write at any angle
  - In two hands
- Digital versions
  - can't replace these
  - replace only the easy to digitalize ones

#### Data format
- Tools with notes as rows with columns (Notion, Holon)
  - Gain: Order
  - Give up: Freedom to not know what it is
- Capture first tools (Apple Notes, Sticky Notes)

#### Data visualization
- Display data as any (rows, tree, calendar, ...)
  - Gain: Slice and dice
  - Give up: Things having a location (whether you want it or not, paper on desk) as data moves all the time
- Arranging things is part of thinking
  - Where something is tells you how urgent (on keyboard -> very urgent)
- Search: What was it called?
- Location: Where did I leave it?
- You can reconstruct places in software (e.g. dashboard, specific page)
- Trade-off: Paper gives you the place for free, in DB-style PKMS you have to create the place
  - Time is the "place" in journals view: When did I do it instead of where did I put it

#### Guilt
- Backlog
  - Sounds like a gift, but acts like debt
  - Not laziness, but the shape of a container with no bottom
  - Gain: Total recall
- Give up: Forcing you to choose

#### Ownership
- Cloud: Company has the data, you get a view
- Gain: Backups, collaborate, access from everywhere
- Give up: You become a borrower of your work -> Local-first

#### Tool-shaped objects
- Things that make you feel productive
  - Larger market

#### User perspective
- Almost no-one choses a tool by its trade-offs
  - Herbert Simon: We decide by "satisfice"
- We take the first option that looks good enough and then stop looking

#### Agents
- All steps become optional: capture, triage, schedule, do
- Ben Shneiderman
  - Direct manipulation (UI visible, you act, it reacts)
  - Wary of automation: Lose predictability, control, understanding

#### Ergo
- Each layer gives something up https://youtu.be/OuEKdD_1F8s?si=XovAPAZUq0d8qjAu&t=858
- Maes: Not every task deserves your attention
- Eric Horvitz: User and machine take turns

### Take-aways
- Have a central place that can't be shoved away (e.g. a virtual desktop)
  - You can jump anywhere easily
  - You can lay it out: Put the things you want/need to do in the center
  - You can't have multiple of them
  - It always starts at the same place
- Blocks are actually only one trade-off
  - Gain: Hierarchy
  - Give up: Free placement
